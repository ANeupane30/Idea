An offtopic note on tomato gardening

This offtopic note is about growing tomatoes, not databases. Tomatoes need full sun for at least six hours a day, and the soil should drain well so the roots never sit in water. Plant seedlings deep, burying part of the stem, because the buried stem grows extra roots and makes the plant sturdier.

Water at the base of the plant in the morning, so the leaves dry during the day and fungal disease has less chance to spread. A layer of mulch keeps the soil moist and cool, and it stops soil from splashing onto the lower leaves when it rains. Feed the plants with a balanced fertiliser when the first fruit sets, then every few weeks after that.

Tall varieties need a stake or a cage. Tie the main stem loosely as it grows, and pinch out the side shoots that appear where a leaf meets the stem, so the plant puts its energy into fruit. Pick the tomatoes when they are fully coloured and slightly soft, and keep them out of the fridge so they keep their flavour.

At the end of the season, pull up the plants and compost the healthy ones. Rotate where you grow tomatoes each year, because the same bed used year after year builds up pests and diseases in the soil. Next spring, start again with fresh seedlings and a bed that has rested.
